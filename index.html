Sovereign AI × Public Good

Mapping control, influence and opportunity across the AI stack
“What if we could make the public good an active input into AI innovation, rather than an outcome we attempt to protect after the fact?”
Control is who can change a system directly, and what they can change.
Influence is who shapes the decisions of the people who hold control.
Foundation: Jensen Huang’s five-layer AI cake: energy, chips, infrastructure, models, applications. The overlay of control and influence is our working lens, not an industry standard.

Sovereignty is not necessarily about owning every layer. It is the degree of meaningful control, operational independence and freedom from unwanted dependencies across the stack. Sovereignty does not by itself produce public benefit.

The New Guard Working concept

Emerging leaders and organizations that seek to advance technological innovation and public benefit together. No one is named, and no movement is claimed. The philosophy should evolve through this conversation.

It isA way to find people already connecting technology to public-interest outcomes. Complementary to research, advocacy, law, regulation and philanthropy. People can work in both approaches.
It is notA faction, a list of opponents, a replacement for traditional institutions, or a claim that a movement already exists.
Established approach: research, advocacy, legislation, policy, regulation, institutional accountability.
Emerging approach (hypothesis): engaging developers directly, technical evaluations, adoption decisions, customer incentives and real-world feedback loops.

Who might we look for?

  • AI developers and researchers
  • Hardware and infrastructure leaders
  • EdTech founders and product leaders
  • Educators and educational institutions
  • Philanthropic leaders and public-interest researchers
  • Organizations making AI adoption decisions
  • Representatives of affected communities
